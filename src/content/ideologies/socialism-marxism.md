Socialism is a family of political and economic ideas united by the conviction that the means of production, such as land, factories and capital, should be owned or controlled collectively rather than privately, so that society's wealth serves everyone rather than a propertied few. Early socialists such as Robert Owen, Charles Fourier and Henri de Saint-Simon imagined cooperative communities. In the mid-nineteenth century **Karl Marx** and Friedrich Engels transformed socialism into a comprehensive theory of history, economics and revolution, and their followers, including **Rosa Luxemburg**, debated how that theory should be put into practice.

## Core ideas

- **Historical materialism.** The way a society produces its material life (its economic "base") shapes its politics, law, religion and ideas (its "superstructure").
- **Class struggle.** History is driven by conflict between classes with opposed interests: masters and slaves, lords and serfs, and in the modern era capitalists (the bourgeoisie) and wage-workers (the proletariat).
- **Exploitation and alienation.** Under capitalism workers are paid less than the value they produce, and they lose control over their work, its products and their own creative nature.
- **Crisis and transformation.** Capitalism is enormously productive but unstable, prone to recurring crises. Marx believed it would give way to a classless society organised around human need.

## Divisions

Socialists have always disagreed fiercely about how to reach their goal. Reformist social democrats sought change through elections and the welfare state. Revolutionaries argued that capitalism could only be overthrown. Within revolutionary Marxism, Luxemburg defended democracy and mass spontaneity against Lenin's tightly disciplined party.

## Legacy

Marxist ideas inspired the labour movement, trade unions and democratic socialist parties across the world. They also inspired the revolutionary regimes of the Soviet Union, China and elsewhere, whose authoritarianism and economic failures, and the millions of deaths under some of them, remain central to arguments over Marx's legacy. As an analytical tool, Marx's attention to class, economic power and ideology has influenced sociology, history, economics and literary criticism far beyond political movements.
