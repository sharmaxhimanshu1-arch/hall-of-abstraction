"Eastern philosophy" is a broad label of convenience for traditions that are as varied among themselves as anything in the West. This Hall groups three of the most influential: **Confucianism** and **Daoism**, which emerged in China during the turbulent Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, and **Buddhism**, which arose in northern India at about the same time. Together they have shaped the ethics, politics and spiritual life of billions of people for more than two and a half millennia.

## Core ideas

- **Confucianism: the cultivated person in a harmonious society.** Moral life begins with the family and extends outward. Through learning, ritual propriety (*li*) and humaneness (*ren*), anyone can become a *junzi*, an exemplary person, and good government rests on rulers' virtue rather than punishment.
- **Daoism: harmony with the Way.** The Dao, the nameless source and pattern of all things, cannot be captured in words. The sage lives simply and spontaneously, practising *wu wei*, effortless, non-coercive action, in the way water yields and yet wears away stone.
- **Buddhism: the end of suffering.** Life as ordinarily lived is marked by *dukkha*, suffering or unsatisfactoriness, which arises from craving. By seeing clearly that all things are impermanent and without a fixed self, and by following the Noble Eightfold Path, one can reach *nirvana*, the extinguishing of craving.

## A shared moment

The German philosopher Karl Jaspers called the period around 800 to 200 BCE the **Axial Age**. In it Confucius and Laozi in China, the Buddha and the Upanishadic sages in India, the Hebrew prophets, and the Greek philosophers, apparently independently, began asking reflective questions about the self, ethics and the cosmos. The idea is debated, but it captures something striking: Socrates was born less than a decade after Confucius died.

## Legacy

Confucianism became the official ideology of the Chinese state for some two thousand years and shaped Korea, Japan and Vietnam. Daoism influenced Chinese medicine, art and religion, and with Buddhism gave rise to Chan, known in Japan as Zen. Buddhism spread across Asia and, in the modern era, to the West, where its meditative practices have inspired mindfulness movements and dialogue with psychology and neuroscience.
